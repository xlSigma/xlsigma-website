Wealth Management Analytics: Turning Fragmented Data into Strategic Growth Priorities

A reusable analytical decision model that evaluated 52 initiatives and shaped a multiyear growth roadmap.

Outcome

The bank implemented the full assessment and met or exceeded its targets over the following five years.

Leadership of a large North American bank's U.S. wealth management business needed to decide where to invest to accelerate growth across its retail, commercial, and brokerage-partner channels, in alignment with the bank's strategic objectives. The assessment translated that broad question into a structured analytical approach, integrating and validating frontline issues, client requirements, process maturity ratings, and leader-set weightings in one interactive, reusable decision model.

The analysis showed that the biggest growth constraints were in client identification, referrals, and onboarding, not in the servicing activities that generated the most complaints. The model let leadership score 52 initiatives on impact, client value, feasibility, strategic alignment, and risk. It produced a prioritized multiyear roadmap.

Chapter 10

The work, step by step

1

The business question

Grow the wealth vertical by embedding it in the channels the bank already had, and aligned with its strategic objectives. Leadership needed to know where to invest first and in what order.
AFrame priority questions with strategy and business leaders

The wealth business sat beside a large retail and commercial bank and a strategic brokerage partner. Each had clients who belonged in wealth, but referrals were manual, client data was fragmented across platforms, and incentives did not reward cross-business work.

The leadership team had a long list of ideas and no common way to compare them. The assessment was set up to answer three questions.

  • Where are the gaps? Which parts of the client journey and which business functions limit growth most?
  • What should we fund? Which initiatives close those gaps with the best balance of impact, effort, and client value?
  • In what order? How do we sequence the work against risk, dependencies, and the bank's integration strategy?
Scope7 client lifecycle phases, 6 process stages, 10 business functions, 5 client segments, and stakeholders across wealth, retail, commercial, operations, technology, and finance.
2

Structuring the problem

One shared map of the client lifecycle gave every team the same language for gaps, data, and fixes.
ABring structure to a broad business question

Every pain point, requirement, and initiative in the assessment was tagged to this framework. That tagging is what made the later heat maps, rollups, and roadmap possible, because every number traces back to a place in the client journey.

3

4

Diagnosis

The biggest capability gaps sat at the front of the client journey, which is not where most complaints were logged.
BCIdentify trends, opportunities, and risks; business-facing reporting

Each function was rated on 7 to 11 process dimensions, such as integration, reporting capability, and people effectiveness, on a 1 to 10 scale. Scores below 3 flagged a gap and scores above 6 a strength.

So whatRanking by complaint volume alone would have pointed investment at servicing. The weighted view showed the growth constraint was upstream: the bank was not identifying, referring, and onboarding wealth clients well. That reframing set the priorities for everything that followed.
5

Prioritization

52 initiatives scored on ease, impact, and client value. A clear top ten emerged, led by front-end data and referral fixes.
CAnalytical tools that deliver decision-ready insight

Each initiative was scored for speed and affordability (1 to 9, higher is easier) and impact (1 to 9). The composite priority score multiplied ease and impact by the client-requirement score and the function weight from chapter 3, then was indexed so the top initiative equals 100.

6

Strategic alignment

The plan was tested against nine integration levers, so it reinforced enterprise strategy rather than compete with it.
ACPartner with strategy; translate findings into recommendations

The bank's integration strategy rested on seven operational levers plus two enterprise levers. Every initiative was rated 0 to 9 on how strongly it moved each lever, then averaged by function.

7

Risk

Risk scoring separated quick wins from the few strategic bets that needed sponsorship and time.
BCIdentify risks tied to strategic initiatives

Each initiative was rated 1 to 9 on technology complexity, time, cost, process complexity, and the number of lines of business involved. The five ratings were summed into a risk score from 5 to 45.

8

Roadmap

Quick wins first, structural change second, innovation third. The bank delivered all three.
ABalance immediate decision support with scalable capability

A multi-generation plan sequenced the work by priority, risk, and dependency. Generation one closed the front-end gaps quickly and freed capacity. Generation two built the data and platform foundations. Generation three used those foundations to compete in new ways.

ResultThe bank implemented the full assessment. Over the next five years the business met or exceeded every target it set.
9

Current-state maturity

An 18-dimension baseline showed strong market opportunity and a strong leadership model, held back by low digitization and automation.
BInterpret complex data for senior leaders

Leaders rated the business from 1 to 10 across three lenses. The profile is lopsided in a useful way: the ingredients for growth were present, and the gaps were concentrated in the delivery engine.

10

From analysis to reusable asset

The assessment was built as a decision model, not a one-time report. Today the same model translates directly to a governed semantic model on a modern BI stack.
DWell-documented, reusable analytical data assets

Every element links to the next: a pain point maps to a function and lifecycle phase, inherits that function's weight and client requirements, and rolls up to an initiative, a lever, and a roadmap generation. Change one input, such as a function weight or an impact score, and the rankings and roadmap update. That traceability is what let leaders challenge the results and trust them.
